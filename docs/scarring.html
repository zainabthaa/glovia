What Causes Scarring?

Scars form when the skin heals after injury or inflammation. They can result from acne, surgery, trauma, or skin conditions. Understanding the type of scar is key to choosing the most effective treatment path.

Acne Scars

Acne scars include indentations (like ice-pick or boxcar scars) or raised areas left behind after breakouts. They often persist long after acne has healed.

Tip: Look for products with retinoids, AHAs, or niacinamide to promote skin renewal and texture smoothing.

Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)

Not true scars, but dark spots left behind by inflammation or breakouts. PIH fades over time but can take months without treatment.

Tip: Use brightening agents like vitamin C, azelaic acid, and sunscreen daily to speed up fading.

Keloid Scars

Keloids are thick, raised scars that grow beyond the original wound area. They often occur after acne, piercings, or injuries, especially in people with darker skin tones.

Tip: Consider products with silicone, onion extract, or consult a dermatologist for steroid injections or laser treatment.
